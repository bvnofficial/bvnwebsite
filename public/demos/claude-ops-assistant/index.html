Section 02

The six month old policy problem

This is the failure your post describes, and it is a content pipeline problem more than a prompting problem. Four things fix it, and all four have to be in place.

1 One place a policy can live

  • A single SharePoint library is the source. Nothing is pasted into a prompt or a Project by hand.
  • Every document carries owner, version, effective date and review date as real columns
  • If it is not in the library, the assistant does not know it exists

2 Reindex on change, not on a calendar

  • Graph delta or a webhook fires the moment a file is edited
  • The old chunks are deleted, not just superseded, so the stale text is physically gone
  • Reindex finishes in minutes, so there is no long window where two versions are live

3 Answers have to cite

  • Every chunk carries its policy ID, version and effective date into the prompt
  • The answer must name them, and the manager sees them at the bottom of the digest
  • A wrong answer becomes visibly wrong instead of quietly wrong

4 Stale content gets caught by the system

  • Anything past its review date is reported weekly to the owner
  • Expired policy is served with a warning banner or withheld, your call per policy type
  • A monthly diff report shows what changed and what got reindexed

Section 04

The prompt structure, not a vibe

Reference material goes in tagged and dated. The instruction to refuse is as important as the instruction to answer.

// system prompt, trimmed You review daily store reports for a multi location retail and wellness operator. Your reader is a district manager reading on a phone. Lead with the verdict. <rules> Answer only from <policy> blocks below. Every compliance claim must name policy_id and effective_date. If no policy block covers it, say so and stop. Do not infer. Never soften a cash handling or safety finding. </rules> <policy id="POL-CASH-004" version="3.1" effective="2026-05-01" owner="Ops"> ...retrieved chunk text... </policy> <report store="118" date="2026-08-06"> ...normalised rows... </report> // output contract, validated in n8n before send { "items": [ { "store", "severity", "finding", "policy_id", "effective_date" } ], "withheld": [ { "reason" } ] }

Section 06

Where I would tell you not to trust it

! The honest list

  • Anything final on people. Discipline, termination, pay disputes. The assistant can surface the policy and the pattern. A human decides.
  • Arithmetic on money. Totals, variances and reconciliations get calculated in code and handed to the model as facts, never computed by it.
  • Anything legal or safety critical without a named human sign off. Draft yes, send no.
  • Brand new policy in its first week. Watch it, sample the answers, then let it run unattended.
  • I do not build systems that are never wrong. I build systems where wrong is visible, logged, and cheap to fix.
